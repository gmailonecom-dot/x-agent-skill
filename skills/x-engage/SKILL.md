# x-engage

On X you grow by being useful in other people's replies, not by posting into the void. The accounts
worth your time are the ones where a good reply reaches their audience and gets you a follow — the
big accounts in your field, and the peers who will become the people you actually know.

This skill reads. It never logs in as the user and never touches their credentials.

## Before you write

1. Read `~/.claude/x/voice.md` if it exists. If it does not, ask for **five of their own posts that
   did well** and write it.
2. Never invent a number, a result or a source. Ask for it or write the line without it.

## Build the roster first

Ask for the user's field and a handful of handles they already respect. Then sort the accounts into
three tiers and say which tier to spend the hour on:

1. **Big accounts** (their audience is bigger than yours). A reply here is the highest reach per
   minute, but only if it adds something. Sorted by how open they are to replies, not by size.
2. **Peers** (roughly your size, same field). These are the relationships that compound. Sorted by
   how often they reply back.
3. **Aspirational** (where you want to be in a year). Watch, reply rarely, and only when you can be
   genuinely useful.

## The reply that earns a follow

For each account, find a recent post and draft ONE reply:

- Add the thing the post did not say — a counter-example, a number from your own work, a correction.
- Never "great post", never an emoji-only reply, never a pitch.
- Under 30 words. Lead with the value, not with agreement.
- No links. A link in a reply is an advert.
- Reply within the first hour if the user can. Say when the window is.

## What to hand back

- the roster, in the three tiers, with one line on why each is worth the hour
- for the top three accounts, the post to reply to and the drafted reply
- a one-line rule the user can keep: what their replies should always add

## The gate

Nothing here publishes. This skill writes and you publish. Every output ends in a block the user
copies, and the last line of every run is the question: **ship it, or change it?**
